Question pool six. Original practice questions for Mock exam 6, weighted towards governance and compliance, Phases A, E, F, G and H, and the deliverables that are easy to confuse.

Question 1.
Topic: concepts
Which document of the TOGAF Fundamental Content describes gap analysis, business scenarios and stakeholder management?
A. Architecture Content.
B. Applying the A D M.
C. A D M Techniques.
D. Introduction and Core Concepts.
[THINK]
The answer is C. The A D M Techniques document holds the techniques used while running the A D M, such as gap analysis, business scenarios, stakeholder management and risk management. Applying the A D M covers iteration and levels, and Architecture Content covers the metamodel, artifacts, deliverables and building blocks.

Question 2.
Topic: concepts
Which statement about the Strategic, Segment and Capability levels of the Architecture Landscape is correct?
A. Detail increases from Strategic, through Segment, to Capability Architecture.
B. Detail increases from Capability, through Segment, to Strategic Architecture.
C. All three levels describe the enterprise at the same level of detail.
D. Segment Architecture is the most detailed, as it is used at project level.
[THINK]
The answer is A. Strategic Architecture gives a summary, long term view of the whole enterprise. Segment Architecture works at program or portfolio level. Capability Architecture is the most detailed, and is used at project level.

Question 3.
Topic: concepts
Which statement about the TOGAF Fundamental Content is correct?
A. It contains the topic guides that evolve quickly between editions of the standard.
B. It is the universal, stable core of the standard, applicable to any enterprise.
C. It is limited to the A D M, while governance is covered only by the Series Guides.
D. It is mandatory for certification, but optional for organizations adopting TOGAF.
[THINK]
The answer is B. The Fundamental Content is the core of the TOGAF Standard: universal, stable guidance that applies to any enterprise. The Series Guides give more specific guidance and evolve faster.

Question 4.
Topic: terminology
What does TOGAF call data about data, of any sort and in any media, that describes the characteristics of an entity?
A. A metamodel.
B. Metadata.
C. A model.
D. A repository.
[THINK]
The answer is B. Metadata is data about data. A metamodel is a model that describes how, and with what, the architecture will be described. A model is a simplified representation of a subject of interest, and a repository is a store for architecture outputs.

Question 5.
Topic: terminology
In TOGAF terms, what is a Transition Architecture?
A. The most detailed level of the Architecture Landscape, used for projects.
B. The architecture of the enterprise as it currently exists today.
C. An architecture for a single program, aligned with the enterprise strategy.
D. A formal description of the enterprise at an architecturally significant point in time.
[THINK]
The answer is D. A Transition Architecture shows the enterprise at an architecturally significant state between the baseline and the target. Option A describes a Capability Architecture, option B the Baseline Architecture, and option C a Segment Architecture.

Question 6.
Topic: terminology
Which of the following is a logical element, rather than a physical one?
A. An Architecture Building Block, defined independently of any product.
B. A Solution Building Block, implemented with a selected vendor product.
C. A specific database server, installed in the company's data center.
D. A purchased software package, deployed for the finance department.
[THINK]
The answer is A. Logical elements are implementation independent, such as Architecture Building Blocks. Solution Building Blocks, specific servers and purchased packages are physical: they are real products and components.

Question 7.
Topic: terminology
What does TOGAF call a qualitative statement of intent that should be met by the architecture, with at least a supporting rationale and a measure of importance?
A. An architecture requirement.
B. An architecture principle.
C. An architecture constraint.
D. An architecture viewpoint.
[THINK]
The answer is B. An architecture principle is a qualitative statement of intent. A requirement is a statement of need, a constraint is an external factor that rules out approaches, and a viewpoint defines how to build a view.

Question 8.
Topic: adm
Which group of iteration cycles covers Phases G and H?
A. Architecture Context iterations.
B. Architecture Definition iterations.
C. Transition Planning iterations.
D. Architecture Governance iterations.
[THINK]
The answer is D. Architecture Governance iterations cover Phases G and H. Architecture Context covers the Preliminary Phase and Phase A, Architecture Definition covers Phases B, C and D, and Transition Planning covers Phases E and F.

Question 9.
Topic: adm
The current state is well understood and stable, and the team wants to base the target on it. Which approach to architecture definition fits best?
A. Baseline first.
B. Target first.
C. Greenfield first.
D. Transition first.
[THINK]
The answer is A. Baseline first suits a current state that is well understood and agreed, so the target can build on it. Target first suits a current state that is complex, poorly understood or disputed.

Question 10.
Topic: adm
Which statement about the A D M is correct?
A. The phases must always be completed in sequence, and each phase exactly once.
B. It can only be applied once the whole enterprise has been documented in detail.
C. It is iterative, over the whole process, between phases and within each phase.

The answer is C. The A D M is iterative: over the whole cycle, between phases, and within a phase. It must be adapted to the organization, and it works alongside project management rather than replacing it.

Question 20.
Topic: phases
In Phase H, a change improves the architecture but adds no new business model and touches only one stakeholder. How is it most likely handled?
A. Through a new Request for Architecture Work and a full A D M cycle.
B. By re-entering the A D M at Phase B to redesign the Business Architecture.
C. Through change management, without re-entering the A D M.
D. By granting a permanent dispensation from the Architecture Board.
[THINK]
The answer is C. A change that affects only one stakeholder is a candidate for change management. A change that affects two or more stakeholders is likely to need architecture redesign and a new pass through the A D M.

Question 30.
Topic: techniques
In which order are the steps of a business scenario performed?
A. Objectives, problem, environment, actors, roles, refine.
B. Problem, environment, objectives, actors, roles, refine.
C. Environment, actors, problem, objectives, refine, roles.
D. Actors, roles, problem, environment, objectives, refine.
[THINK]
The answer is B. A business scenario first identifies the problem, then the business and technical environment, then the desired objectives, then the human and computer actors, then the roles, responsibilities and measures of success, and finally checks fitness for purpose and refines the result.

Question 32.
Topic: techniques
In TOGAF risk management, how is the impact of a risk determined?
A. From its effect and its frequency.
B. From its cost and its owner.
C. From its phase and its domain.
D. From its residual value and its initial value.
[THINK]
The answer is A. TOGAF classifies a risk by its effect, from catastrophic to negligible, and its frequency, from frequent to unlikely. Together these give the impact. Initial and residual describe a risk before and after mitigation.

Question 33.
Topic: governance
An implementation implements every feature of the specification in accordance with it, and nothing more. What is its conformance level?
A. Compliant.
B. Conformant.
C. Consistent.
D. Fully conformant.
[THINK]
The answer is D. Fully conformant means a full match: every feature in the specification is implemented in accordance with it, and there are no extra features. Conformant means all specification features plus extras. Compliant means some features are missing and there are no extras.

Question 34.
Topic: governance
An implementation leaves out some features of the specification. Everything it does implement is in the specification, and is implemented correctly. What is its conformance level?
A. Compliant.
B. Consistent.
C. Conformant.
D. Non-conformant.
[THINK]
The answer is A. Compliant means some specification features are missing, but nothing extra was added and everything implemented follows the specification. Consistent would also add features outside the specification.

Question 35.
Topic: governance
Which of the following is a responsibility of the Architecture Board?
A. Writing the code for the Solution Building Blocks of each project.
B. Granting dispensations and enforcing architecture compliance.
C. Approving the enterprise's yearly financial statements.
D. Selecting the vendor for every procurement in the enterprise.
[THINK]
The answer is B. The Architecture Board enforces architecture compliance, grants dispensations, keeps the architectures consistent, and monitors Architecture Contracts. It does not build solutions or run procurement and finance.
