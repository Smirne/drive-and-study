TOGAF Foundation. Episode five. Phases E to H, and Requirements Management.

Phase E. Opportunities and Solutions. This is the first phase directly concerned with implementation: how the target architecture will actually be delivered. The objectives: generate the initial complete version of the Architecture Roadmap, based upon the gap analysis and candidate roadmap components from Phases B, C and D; and determine whether an incremental approach is required, and if so, identify Transition Architectures that will deliver continuous business value.

In Phase E you consolidate the gaps from all domains, review the requirements and constraints, consider the enterprise's readiness and the interoperability requirements, identify the major work packages, and group them into projects. A work package is a set of actions identified to achieve one or more objectives for the business. Phase E is also where you confirm the implementation and migration strategy: for example a greenfield approach, a revolutionary approach, or an evolutionary one; and whether to replace, reuse, or buy. Remember: Phase E creates the first version of the Architecture Roadmap and introduces the Transition Architectures.

Phase F. Migration Planning. The objectives: finalize the Architecture Roadmap and the supporting Implementation and Migration Plan; ensure that the Implementation and Migration Plan is coordinated with the enterprise's approach to managing and implementing change in its overall change portfolio; and ensure that the business value and cost of work packages and Transition Architectures is understood by key stakeholders.

So Phase F is where the plan becomes final and is aligned with the enterprise's portfolio and project management. Activities include confirming management framework interactions, assigning a business value to each work package, estimating resource requirements, timings and availability, prioritizing the migration projects through cost benefit and risk validation, and confirming the Architecture Roadmap. A useful distinction: Phase E answers what are the possible solutions and how they group; Phase F answers in which order, at what cost, and with what value. Phase F typically ends this ADM cycle's planning and also includes a step to conduct lessons learned.

Phase G. Implementation Governance. The objectives: ensure conformance with the Target Architecture by implementation projects; and perform appropriate architecture governance functions for the solution and any implementation driven architecture change requests.

The key word here is conformance. Architects don't implement; they govern implementation. The central deliverable is the Architecture Contract, a joint agreement between the development partners and the sponsors on the deliverables, quality, and fitness for purpose of an architecture. Phase G also includes architecture compliance reviews, and produces Compliance Assessments and Change Requests. Implementation projects themselves are run by the enterprise's normal project management.

Phase H. Architecture Change Management. The objectives: ensure that the architecture lifecycle is maintained; ensure that the Architecture Governance Framework is executed; and ensure that the Enterprise Architecture Capability meets current requirements.

Phase H is about keeping the architecture alive. It monitors technology changes and business changes, and it decides how to respond to a change request. Here's an important exam point: TOGAF classifies architecture changes into three categories. A simplification change, which can normally be handled through change management techniques. An incremental change, which may be capable of being handled through change management, if properly managed. And a re-architecting change, which requires putting the whole architecture through the architecture development cycle again, starting a new A D M cycle with a new Request for Architecture Work. Simplification, incremental, re-architecting. Note also that a change request is driven by drivers such as new technology, asset management cost reductions, or new business requirements.

Now, Requirements Management, at the centre of the circle. Its objectives: ensure that the Requirements Management process is sustained and operates for all relevant A D M phases; manage architecture requirements identified during any execution of the A D M cycle or a phase; and ensure that relevant architecture requirements are available for use by each phase as the phase is executed.

Important points. Requirements Management is not static; it is a dynamic process. Requirements change throughout the cycle, and every phase both consumes and produces requirements. Note also that TOGAF does not mandate a specific requirements tool or process. Requirements are stored in the Architecture Requirements Repository, which is part of the Architecture Repository. And the A D M is continuously driven by the requirements management process. Business scenarios are an important technique for discovering requirements.

Let's review the key deliverables across the cycle, in order of appearance. Request for Architecture Work: sent by the sponsor, triggers the cycle. Statement of Architecture Work: approved in Phase A, defines scope and approach. Architecture Vision: Phase A. Architecture Definition Document and Architecture Requirements Specification: started in A, completed in B, C and D. Architecture Roadmap: initial version in E, finalized in F. Implementation and Migration Plan: started in E, finalized in F. Transition Architectures: identified in E, defined in E and F. Architecture Contract: Phase G. Compliance Assessment: Phase G. Change Request: G and H.

Quick self test.

In which phase is the initial complete version of the Architecture Roadmap generated?
[PAUSE]
Phase E, Opportunities and Solutions.

Which phase ensures the Implementation and Migration Plan is coordinated with the enterprise's change portfolio?
[PAUSE]
Phase F, Migration Planning.

What are the three categories of architecture change in Phase H?
[PAUSE]
Simplification, incremental, and re-architecting. A re-architecting change requires a new A D M cycle.

What is an Architecture Contract?
[PAUSE]
A joint agreement between development partners and sponsors on the deliverables, quality, and fitness for purpose of an architecture. It is central to Phase G.

Where are architecture requirements stored?
[PAUSE]
In the Architecture Requirements Repository, part of the Architecture Repository.
